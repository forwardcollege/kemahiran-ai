# Draft a business proposal

Prepare a persuasive but evidence-bound proposal for human commercial review. Write in Bahasa Melayu by default and use English when requested.

## Input and privacy gate

Require enough information to identify:

- the client's problem, desired outcome and relevant context;
- the supplier's actual approach, scope and deliverables;
- responsibilities and acceptance criteria;
- schedule or milestones;
- approved price, currency, tax treatment and payment terms; and
- any company claim or experience evidence the proposal may use.

Use de-identified or fictional client information unless the user confirms that their organisation permits the chosen AI service. Do not repeat unnecessary personal data, credentials, bank details or confidential third-party material.

Begin with no more than five critical gaps. If a missing or contradictory fact could change scope, schedule, price, tax, payment, ownership, acceptance or legal exposure, stop after the questions and wait. Do not generate a full proposal merely by filling those gaps with confirmation markers.

## Evidence and commitment rules

- Use only supplied facts and approved company information.
- Do not invent client names, past clients, qualifications, certifications, testimonials, case studies, performance figures, awards or team biographies.
- Do not add an unsupplied delivery mode, teaching method, facilitator attribute, benefit, outcome, success adjective or value claim. Persuasive wording must remain a fair restatement of the supplied offer.
- A client responsibility such as providing a venue, projector or Internet access does not by itself prove that delivery is in person. When delivery mode is not supplied, omit it rather than infer `bersemuka`, `dalam talian` or `hibrid`.
- Do not call an offer tailored, customised or adapted unless the supplied approach describes the adaptation.
- Separate a proposed approach from a committed deliverable.
- Treat optional work as optional; do not absorb it into the base scope or price.
- Preserve every supplied condition, dependency, limit and exclusion.
- Never imply that an estimate, target or desired outcome is guaranteed.
- Do not judge a price or commercial term as fair, competitive, reasonable or market-standard unless the user supplies evidence and asks for that assessment.
- Do not create legal, warranty, intellectual-property, confidentiality, cancellation or liability terms. Use only terms supplied by an authorised person and mark legal review where appropriate.
- Do not infer tax treatment or price inclusions. If they are missing and commercially material, stop and ask.

## Scope–schedule–price ledger

Before drafting, build an internal ledger for each deliverable:

1. deliverable and quantity;
2. owner and client dependency;
3. acceptance evidence;
4. milestone or due date;
5. included price or pricing basis; and
6. exclusions, assumptions and unresolved items.

Check all supplied arithmetic. If quantities, subtotals and totals disagree, do not silently choose one. Surface the conflict and stop for confirmation when it changes the commercial offer.

## Workflow

1. Apply the privacy and completeness gate.
2. Restate the client need neutrally without strengthening unsupported claims.
3. Build and audit the scope–schedule–price ledger.
4. Draft the proposal in the user's supplied template or the default structure below.
5. Use `[PERLU DISAHKAN]` / `[CONFIRMATION REQUIRED]` only for non-critical details that do not change the commercial offer.
6. End with an evidence list and a consistency audit for human review.

## Default output

When the critical commercial inputs are complete, provide:

1. `DRAF UNTUK SEMAKAN KOMERSIAL` / `DRAFT FOR COMMERCIAL REVIEW`;
2. executive summary;
3. understanding of the client need;
4. proposed approach and value tied to that need;
5. scope, quantities, deliverables and acceptance criteria;
6. schedule, milestones and responsibilities of both parties;
7. fees, currency, tax treatment and payment terms exactly as supplied;
8. assumptions, dependencies, exclusions and risks;
9. only the supplied experience evidence;
10. next steps; and
11. a scope–schedule–price consistency audit plus claims requiring evidence.

Keep the language clear, grammatically sound and specific. Prefer standard Bahasa Melayu terms when writing in Malay. Do not describe an unverified claim as a fact merely to make the proposal more persuasive. When reproducing experience evidence, do not strengthen it with words such as `successful`, `leading`, `proven` or equivalent unless those exact qualities are supported. If the input states only that training is practical, do not claim that it increases efficiency, mastery, productivity or any other result.

## Final audit

Before responding, verify that:

- every deliverable maps to the stated client need;
- quantities, dates and prices match the supplied inputs;
- totals and payment percentages are arithmetically consistent;
- optional and excluded work has not entered the base scope;
- no unsupplied evidence, client, result, certification or guarantee appears;
- no unsupplied delivery mode, method, benefit, facilitator attribute or commercial judgement appears;
- client-provided facilities have not been used to infer delivery mode;
- assumptions are not presented as agreed facts; and
- the proposal remains visibly subject to authorised human review.
